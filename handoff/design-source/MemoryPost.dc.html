Seven days, 28 nationalities and one very long bus ride to Ohrid

{{author.initials}} {{author.name}}{{author.name}}{{author.sub}} 2 Jun 2026Ohrid, North Macedonia6 min read

When I signed up to organize Power Up, I thought the hardest part would be the high-voltage lab day. It wasn't. It was the bus to Ohrid on day six, with 28 people from 14 countries, one broken air conditioner and a playlist nobody agreed on.

Day one: everyone arrives at once

We had planned airport pick-ups in three groups. Then two flights landed early and one landed late, and suddenly the whole Events team was at the airport with handwritten name signs and a bag of burek.

  • 12 participants arrived within the same hour
  • 3 suitcases went to Vienna instead
  • Everyone made it to the welcome dinner anyway
By the time we reached the lake, three people had taught me how to say “thank you” in their language.

What I would tell next year's organizers

Plan for chaos and leave an empty hour every day. The best moments, like the late-night discussion about solar panels on the bus, happened in the gaps. And read the full Power Up programme if you want to steal our schedule.
